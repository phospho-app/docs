A **policy** is the brain of your robot. It tells the robot what to do in a given situation. Mathematically, it's a function $$\pi$$ that maps the current **state** $$S$$ of the robot to an **action** $$A$$.

$$
\pi: S \rightarrow A
$$

Policies are the core concept in **reinforcement learning** (learning from rewards) and **imitation learning** (learning by mimicking data). 

# Vision-Language Action Models (VLAs)

The latest paradigm since 2024 in AI robotics are **[Vision-Language Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246) (VLAs)**. They leverage **[Large Language Models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_language_model) (LLMs)** to understand and act on human instructions. You may also hear about [VLM](https://huggingface.co/blog/vlms) (Vision-Language Models).

- VLA models are particularly well-suited for robotics because **they function as a brain**. 
- VLA process both **images** and **text** instructions to predict the next **action**.
- Unlike traditional AI models that generate text (like ChatGPT), these models output actions, such as *move left*.

Essentially, you could prompt your robot to "pick up the red ball" and it would do so.

The [phospho dev kit](https://robots.phospho.ai) helps you learn and experiment with the latest AI robotics models and VLAs.

# What robotics AI models are there for SO-100 robot arms?

Today, **there is no one-size-fits-all model for robotics.** To get the best reuslts, you need to **train** models on your own data, for your specific robot and task.

However, here is a list of **popular AI robotics models** that you can train with the phospho dev kit, which are known to work well with the SO-100 robot arm.

## ACT (Action Chunking Transformer)

[ACT (Action Chunking Transformer)](https://github.com/Shaka-Labs/ACT) is a popular repo that lets you experiment with imitation learning. The idea is that cameras' images are projected into an embeddings space, and then decoded as an action sequence. Learn more [here](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.09246).

![ACT model architecture](/assets/policies-act.png)

**How it works**:
- You record episodes of your robot performing a task.
- The model learns from this data and enacts a policy based on it.

**Why use ACT?**
- Typically requires ~30 episodes for training
- Can run on an RTX 3000 series GPU in less than 30 minutes.
- This is a great starting point to get your hands dirty with AI in robotics.
- You don't need prompts to train the model.

## gr00t-n1 by Nvidia

[GR00T-N1 (Generalist Robot 00 Technology)](https://github.com/NVIDIA/Isaac-GR00T) is NVIDIA's foundation model for humanoid robots. It's a performant models, trained on lots of data, which is ideal for fine tuning. The mode weights [are available on Hugging Face.](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/GR00T-N1-2B). For details, see their [paper on arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.14734)

![GR00T-N1 model architecture](/assets/policies-gr00t.png)

**Key features:**
- Processes natural language instructions, camera feeds, and sensor data to generate actions.
- Based on denoizing of the action space, kind of like a Diffusion transformer.
- Trained on a massive datasets of human movements, 3D environments, and AI-generated data.

**Why use GR00T-N1?**
- Typically requires ~50 episodes for training.
- Supports prompting and zero-shot learning for tasks not explicitly seen during training.
- Small model size (2B parameters) for efficient fine-tuning and fast inference on Nvidia Jetson devices.

## pi0 (pi-zero) by Physical Intelligence

[pi0](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi) refers to a family VLA model by Physical Intelligence, that contains the **π₀** (pi0, pi-Zero) model, a flow-based diffusion vision-language-action model (VLA) and the **π₀-FAST** (pi0-FAST) model, an autoregressive VLA, based on the FAST action tokenizer.

The weight of pi0 are open sourced [on Huggingface](https://huggingface.co/blog/pi0)

![pi0 model architecture](/assets/policies-pi0.png)

**Key differences with GR00T-N1:**
- pi0-FAST is autoregressive, while GR00T-N1 is diffusion-based.
- The tooling is less dependent on Nvidia hardware
- Results are more reproducible (better for research)

## OpenVLA

[OpenVLA](https://github.com/openvla/openvla?tab=readme-ov-file#getting-started) is a great repo to learn about Vision Language Action models. VLA are advanced model designed for **complex robotics tasks**, which includes an LLM in its architecture.

![OpenVLA model architecture](/assets/policies-openvla.png)

**Key differences with ACT:**
- Training such a model requires more data and computational power.
- Typically needs ~100 episodes for training 
- Training takes a few hours on an NVIDIA A100 GPU.

For more details, check out [Nvidia's blog post](https://www.jetson-ai-lab.com/openvla.html) on OpenVLA.

## Diffusion Transformers

**Diffusion transformers** are a family of models based on the **diffusion process**. Instead of deterministically mapping states to actions, the model **hallucinates** (generates) the **most probable next action** based on **patterns learned from data**. There exists small and large models in this family.

![Diffusion transformer model architecture](/assets/policies-rdt.png)

**Why consider Diffusion Transformers?**
- The currently **#1 model in robotics** on Hugging Face is a diffusion transformer called [RDT-1b](https://huggingface.co/robotics-diffusion-transformer/rdt-1b).
- Fine tuning the model on your own data is expensive but inference is fast. 
- The model is promptable

# LeRobot Integration

[LeRobot is a github repo by Hugging Face](https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/tree/main/lerobot/common/policies) which implements training scripts for various policies in a standardized way. Supported policies include:

- act
- diffusion
- pi0
- tdmpc
- vqbet
